The phrasing in "Imperial Inconsistencies Exposed Under Crumbs" would make some critical readers skeptical of the truth of the trends. Perhaps institutions were not weakening and crumbling, but renovating under the rule of Crumbs. Maybe free speech was not entirely under attack: individuals could still say and write whatever, leaving the question of how much visibility and attention that speech garners the only thing at liberty. Computing systems, during that era, had become awfully efficient at filtration, a demanding objective since the United States, at that point in time, had shifted greatly towards a services-dominated economy where going back to the fundamentals of manufacturing was a greater financial risk than an improvement in national economics. Perhaps the United States was not really on a retreat from world affairs in those years. Maybe that fact was just becoming obvious to the civilian population, as other countries broke the iron grip the West, under American military leadership, held over their economic developments. In that expository, the generalities were stated, but there were always cases that refuted those generalities.

If one were to ask a Brittanian the validity of some of the claims made in that fictional expository, the Brittanian would have just laughed in their face, drunk on Parisian wine while being fondled by all flavors of Arabian women, right in the innermost depths of the Brittanian kernel, London. The Brittanian would not have taken those claims seriously. That would have upset their mood, made them fearful of their own futures, futures that were most definitely not to be guaranteed by the graces of their Indian and Arabian viceroys, who had also developed certain appetites in all sorts of things. And that was perhaps the problem with Western leadership during those years. There were problems in the lower and upper echelons of Western civilization. Yet to tell these issues to a Brittanian would be to risk oneself being mocked for being alarmist and chided for being weak by complaining.

Literary devices such as allusions and statements on trends can never get to the entire truth. But they make for a great start in understanding the underlying matters in operation, even when those matters take place in fictional settings. What is fiction in one setting may actually bear real principle to the realities of this world.

The theme of fatalism is not one to be discussed with the weak of heart or clarified with the strong of mind. Fatalism just is, just so happened and will happen. It leads to loss in the future, and uselessness to do anything different in the present.
